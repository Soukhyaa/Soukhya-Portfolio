# Research File — Portfolio Website

**Author:** Soukhya Wale
**Purpose:** Background research used to plan the design and content of the portfolio site.

---

## 1. Why a portfolio matters

For a B.Tech AI & Data Science student, a portfolio site serves three purposes:
- Acts as a single link to share with recruiters, professors, or hackathon organizers instead of scattering info across resume/GitHub/LinkedIn.
- Demonstrates practical web development skill (HTML, CSS, JS, React) alongside AI/data science coursework — showing range, not just one track.
- Gives context that a resume can't: tone, personality, and how you explain your own work.

## 2. What recruiters and reviewers actually look for

Common patterns observed across student/developer portfolio guides:
- **Clarity over decoration.** Name, role, and what you do should be understandable in the first 5 seconds.
- **Working contact info above the fold.** Email and GitHub should never require scrolling or hunting.
- **Real skills, not buzzword soup.** A short, honest skill list reads better than an exaggerated one.
- **Fast load, mobile-friendly.** Many reviewers open portfolio links on their phone first.
- **Personality in small doses.** A subtle signature detail (animation, phrasing, layout choice) makes a portfolio memorable without looking unprofessional.

## 3. Structure choices for a student portfolio (3rd year, no full work history yet)

Since formal work experience is limited at this stage, the structure prioritizes:
1. **Hero** — name + one-line identity statement, so the "who is this" question is answered instantly.
2. **About** — short paragraph connecting the AI/Data Science coursework to hands-on web development, since that combination is the differentiator.
3. **Skills** — a clean, scannable list (HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React, Python) rather than a project showcase, since this is a starting portfolio meant to be expanded later.
4. **Contact** — email and GitHub, the two channels that matter most for outreach.

## 4. Design direction research

Reviewed common AI-generated design defaults to deliberately avoid them (cream background + serif + terracotta; plain dark mode with neon accent used generically; newspaper-style hairline layouts). Instead, the direction leans into a **developer-terminal aesthetic**: monospace accents, a typing-terminal animation in the hero, and a teal accent — reflecting both the "code" and "data" identity of an AI & Data Science student, rather than a generic template look.

## 5. Next steps for expanding this portfolio

- Add a **Projects** section once 2–3 solid projects exist (with GitHub links, live demos if possible).
- Add a **Resume/CV** download button.
- Add a **Certifications** section for any completed courses (e.g. NPTEL, MOOCs).
- Consider adding LinkedIn once a profile is set up, for a second professional contact channel.
